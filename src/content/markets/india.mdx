Sri Lanka is the easiest international trip from South India: a shorter flight than Delhi to Goa, no jet lag, and food that will feel familiar and new at once. Because flights are short, a **long weekend of 4–5 days** genuinely works, and a week lets you see the island's three big themes: ancient cities, tea hills and beaches.

## How much can you do in 5 days?

One region done properly. The most popular short loop is the **Cultural Triangle plus Kandy**: Sigiriya rock, the Dambulla cave temples and Kandy's Temple of the Tooth, all within three hours of the airport. In December–March you can swap this for the **south coast** (Galle Fort, Mirissa, whale watching).

## Which month?

Your holidays are fixed, so pick the side of the island that's dry:

- **Pongal and January long weekends:** the south and west coasts and hill country are at their best. The east coast is still wet.
- **April–May summer holidays:** hot everywhere. The east coast (Trincomalee, Pasikuda) and Cultural Triangle are dry, while the southwest monsoon arrives in the south and west from May.
- **Diwali (October/November):** the trickiest time, with inter-monsoon storms island-wide. The south coast dries out first, from late November. Keep the route short and indoor-friendly: Kandy, Galle Fort, Colombo's food scene.
- **Christmas and New Year:** peak season in the south and hills. Book early.

## Good to know

- **Money:** Indian rupees are not widely accepted. Withdraw Sri Lankan rupees (LKR) from ATMs on arrival; cards work in hotels and larger restaurants.
- **Food:** rice and curry, hoppers, kottu and seafood. Jaffna in the north has strong Tamil culinary links with Tamil Nadu.
