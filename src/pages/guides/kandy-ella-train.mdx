The railway from Kandy through the tea country to Ella is the one journey almost every Sri Lanka itinerary includes. The planner treats it as a **scenic leg**: it's slower than a car (about 6½–7 hours against roughly 4), but the ride is the attraction, so routes are built to include it.

## The route

The line climbs from Kandy (about 500 m) through Peradeniya and Gampola into the tea estates around Hatton and Nanu Oya, the station for Nuwara Eliya. It tops out at **Pattipola, the highest station on the network at about 1,900 m**, then winds down through Ohiya, Haputale and Bandarawela to Ella. The famous **Nine Arch Bridge** is a little beyond Ella towards Demodara, so you won't cross it unless you ride further. Most people walk there from Ella instead.

## Which class?

- **First class (observation or AC):** reserved seats, quieter, fewer open doors and windows.
- **Second and third class reserved:** a fixed seat with open windows, which is the best way to photograph the views.
- **Unreserved second and third:** cheap and often packed in season. Fine for the adventurous; tough with luggage.
- **Tourist specials** (such as the Ella Odyssey) run on some days with tourist-oriented carriages and photo stops.

## Getting tickets

Reserved seats open roughly a month ahead and sell out fast for December–April. Book through Sri Lanka Railways' official online reservation system, at a station counter in person, or through a reseller (at a mark-up, but useful if you're booking from abroad). Check the current schedule and booking window before you plan around it, as both change.

## Tips

- Break the journey at **Nanu Oya** (for Nuwara Eliya) or **Haputale** if you'd like two shorter rides instead of one long one.
- Trains are often late, so don't plan a tight connection at the far end.
- Bring a layer: the highlands are cool, especially in the morning.
- Hang out of the door only if you're comfortable with the risk. Every year there are accidents.
