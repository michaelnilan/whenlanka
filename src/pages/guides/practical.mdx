## Visa (ETA)

Most visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) or visa before arrival. The rules have changed several times in recent years, including periods when the ETA was free for certain nationalities. **Always apply through the official site, [eta.gov.lk](https://www.eta.gov.lk)**, and be wary of look-alike agencies that charge extra.

## Money

- The currency is the **Sri Lankan rupee (LKR)**. ATMs are widespread in towns; withdraw on arrival at the airport.
- Cards are accepted in most hotels and larger restaurants. Carry cash for tuk-tuks, small cafés, temple entrance donations and markets.
- Tipping is appreciated: around 10% where no service charge is added, and a daily tip for a private driver.

## Is Sri Lanka safe?

Sri Lanka is generally a safe and welcoming country for travellers, including solo women, with the usual precautions. The real risks are practical:

- **Roads:** traffic is chaotic and night driving is risky. Hiring a car with an experienced driver is the norm.
- **The sea:** rip currents are strong, especially during the monsoon on each coast. Swim where locals swim and respect red flags. The planner routes you to the calmer coast for your month.
- **Wildlife:** keep your distance from elephants, including on roads near national parks.
- **Scams:** gem shops, "special festival today" detours and overpriced tuk-tuks. Agree tuk-tuk fares first, or use ride-hailing apps in Colombo.

Check your own government's travel advice before you go.

## Getting around

- **Car and driver:** the most flexible option for most itineraries. [Request a quote](/itinerary/14-days#driver).
- **Train:** slow but scenic. See the [Kandy–Ella train guide](/guides/kandy-ella-train).
- **Bus:** very cheap and everywhere, but crowded and fast.
- **Driving yourself:** you'll need an International Driving Permit plus local endorsement, and it isn't recommended for first-timers.

## Phone & internet

Local SIM cards (Dialog, SLT-Mobitel, Hutch and others) are sold at the airport arrivals hall and are cheap. Mobile data coverage is good in towns and along main roads.

## Temples & dress

Cover shoulders and knees, take off shoes and hats at shrines, and never pose for photos with your back to a Buddha image. Buddha tattoos can cause problems; keep them covered. See [Sacred & Silent](/sacred) for etiquette at monasteries.

## Health

Drink bottled or filtered water, use mosquito repellent (dengue occurs year-round, more after rains) and carry sun protection. Check vaccinations with a travel clinic and get travel insurance that covers activities like surfing and safaris.
